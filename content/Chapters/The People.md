## Overview
The peoples of [[The World#Thaja|Thaja]] are varied and diverse.  Centuries of trade, genetic engineering, and migration led to much variability between populations. With a few climactic exceptions, the races of Thaja are largely ubiquitous across her face.  Now, in the wake of imperial collapse, peoples are more often defined by cultural similarities, with appearance and decoration often following form and function.  Race and gender rarely define a person to the same degree that community and geographic region do.  One peculiar near-constant is the prevalence of the humanoid races, both aquatic and terrestrial.  While there are certainly animals, machines, and even whole planets capable of speech, magic, and cognition, most societies have formed from and around humanoids.

These humanoids, however, are as diverse as Thaja's many regions.  Ancient human gene-stock [[The People#Fad e Bosks|Boskers]] in polar *bosks* appear slim and frail like the short conifers they protect, yet are as comfortable in the cold as their solid Oread neighbors.  The Vanara hierarchs of the [[The People#The Coastal Empires|Coastal Empires]] keep half-orc bodyguard retinues who tolerate the humidity in full armor owing to millennia of ancestry there.  Cultural monoliths are rare, even within the remnants of the old [[The People#The Halliger Remnants|Halliger]] empire, but some enduring institutions (e.g. the [[The People#The Sahida|Sahida]], or the [[The People#Fad e Bosks|Fad e Bosks]]) have rigid frameworks which now span millennia.  As such, this section is organized first with the consolidated peoples of the region, and then those organizations which exist outside of centralized governance.

It is often suggested in the discourses of the *Silattvik* that the peoples of Thaja can, by their numbers alone, effect an [[The Magic#Aïnhaz|aïnhaz]], or even some bizarre, gestalt [[The Magic#Haid and Agïs|agïs]].  This is, to any serious scholar, patently false.  Consider for a moment the implication of such an inane theory.  To believe this would imply that a village, a city, or perhaps even a whole people could have some sort of magical ability.  Who would control such an ability?  Such an idea would necessitate that the *agïs* of Thaja herself come from you and I, either robbing us and our souls of agency or Thaja of her identity.

>Incredible. Tarjali philosophy at its finest, blinders worn by insecure, Cala-bound imbeciles who would surcease an insight if it made their own holy personhood porous by the slightest amount.  Encourage the next Tarjali you meet to ask Thaja honestly and with an open heart from where her *padrost* flows.  If they are really so special, perhaps she'll care enough to reply.  If they're so special, perhaps shock they feel at the answer will be felt the whole world over.
## Peoples
### The Tarjal
*Note: this is supposed to be "Tarjali" but I typod it and now there's 100000 links to the typod heading and changing them all will kill me.*

The Tarjali are a mostly decentralized coalition of villages and Calas centered around the glorious city [[The Land#Topaz|Topaz]].  Owing to the challenging geography of the [[The Land#The Region|Tavilla]], populations are necessarily dense, surrounding agrarian communities or water infrastructure (mostly from the river *Suja* or her tributaries), with travels of weeks on foot expected to reach population centers.  The Tarjali system of governance, one of almost complete decentralization, it uniquely suited to their geographical constraints.  As with many elements of Tarjali culture, this organizational anarchy reflects the deep influence of the Sahida, who have had, other than the keepers of the Grut Oerleverbosk in the heart of Topaz, the longest permanent foothold in the region.  

When the Halliger empire rose to chase the glaciers back to the poles at the end of *Frest lal Kahbir,* it found a potential technological and magical rival in Topaz. Situated as it was on the other side of Thaja, however, the burgeoning Halliger army was poorly situated for a conquest.  The Tarjali, however, had no interest in war, and were largely content to keep to their ways while flying the Halliger flag.  The Halliger emperors, ever conscious of the threat that could be posed by Topaz, the Sahida strongholds, and many of the oldest Boskers if they united, worked to peacefully assimilate the Tarjali for centuries while building nearly impregnable but modest sattelite fortresses in the region.  Probably it was the empire's myopic focus on preventing astronomical advancement and the development of interplanar magics which likely saved the Tarjali from increased attention.  Many of the Tarjali's famous and defining inventions, most notably the *qatila*, never spread beyond the Tavilla and the Ars'Dadshul.  Despite this, the loose leash and relative autonomy granted to the Tarjali would plant the seeds of independence that the Coastal Empires across the Ars'Dadshul would leverage millennia later, ultimately spelling the end of the Halliger empire.

The *Calas* are the cornerstone of the Tarjali system.   Each *Cala* forms the center of an administrative region, and each is largely autonomous.  Topaz is recognized as the highest government of the Tarjali, but individual *Calas* can and do buck Topazian laws and traditions.  Local wazzili manage disagreements, and if they are unable to arbitrate or their decision isn't respected, the dispute is typically remanded to the Sahida. In the rare (but present) case where a disagreement between a *Cala* and Topaz reaches a boiling point, it typically falls on the Sahida again to arbitrate.  Should this fail, Calas are well within their rights to claim a military solution, typically a duel or a staged non-lethal skirmish.  These days, however, the might of Topaz (and importantly its control over much of the region's water) means these disputes are rarely resolved this way.  The resilience of this system is, it is widely agreed, much more to do with the enduring wisdom of the Sahida than any statecraft on the part of Topaz or the Calas.

On a local scale, administration works in much the same way.  Individual villages are also autonomous, as are the citizens within them.  In place of the rigid laws common within the, say, the Coastal Empires, the Tarjali have *wazzili*.  It is the function of a *wazzil* to mediate a discussion between aggrieved parties and negotiate a resolution which best meets the needs of the village and the parties. There are arbitrations common enough to have wrote results and typical compensations for certain harms.  This information is recorded in the *Qanunasad*, a well updated reference created in conjunction with the Sahida to aid *wazzili* in their duties.  Nearly every village has a *wazzil*, and every *Cala* maintains one *wazzil* for every 960 citizens in its jurisdiction.  This archaic but rigid guideline is aimed at ensuring every *wazzil* personally knows all parties in a dispute, the reasoning being that if a *wazzil* speaks to just two new people a day, in one year they will have met 960 people.  It is a noble but often unfulfilled ambition.  In practice, the amount of time spent resolving disputes means a *wazzil* has far fewer than 480 free days.  

For the Tarjali, water, and particularly the river *Suja*, has always been a large facet of daily life.  During *Frest lal Kahbir,* *Suja* was the lifeline through the snowbound wastes of the [[The Land#The Region|Ars'Dadshul]] to the sea.  As the snow melted, the pastoral plains that replaced them suited the hunting of migratory fauna, but not the rooted lifestyle of the Tarjali.  Instead, the agrarian Tarjali clung to the banks of *Suja* and her tributaries.  There is a perjorative term ("chenu thatchya", lit. "one who doesn't value life") for Tarjali who, through choice or penance, are forced to live in areas with no fresh, available water.  The related term "chenu kop'man", lit. "one who sacrifices life", is used to describe those Badlanders who live in these areas in service of the greater good.

It is a related Tarjali belief that to submerge oneself entirely in deep water is a sublime luxury. Travelers to Topaz or large *Calas* are often stunned by the *Maqubahrya,* grand underground cisterns, wide as a city and dappled with light from strategically placed glass lenses from above.  These huge, humid spaces with cool, deep water fed by ingenious filtration systems from the cities above are incomprehensible to those who do not hail from advanced city states.  What is incomprehensible to nearly every visitor is that these cavernous spaces, filled with sculpture, horticultural displays, and secluded corners, are accessible to anyone.  It is considered an extreme punishment to deprive a Tarjali access to the *Maqubahrya.*  It is not uncommon to hear that someone has chosen exile over revocation of the privileges of the cisterns.  

This egalitarian attitude towards public pleasure has resulted in some singular cultural traits.  The Tarjali's well bathed, highly social urban populace contrasts oddly with its insular, dusty, rural communities.  Cultural intermixing is achieved by a common coming-of-age ritual, wherein *cala* dwellers travel to villages in a vocational capacity, often using their skills in stone-shaping to assist in the development of village infrastructure.  Village dwellers go the reverse direction, sometimes trekking weeks, typically escorted by wandering Sahida warriors, to the nearest *cala*, wherein they are granted free passage by qatila to Topaz and provided a year of lodging, education, and modest pay, along with a copy of the Khulasad.  While there is, formally, no expectation for newly minted adult Tarjali to return home from their journeys, family ties often bring villagers back to the villages, and vice versa, now with a small sum to begin their new life or profession.  In this way, the rural villages, often highly fortified against the ever present threat of monsters, contain more of the character of the city than is clear at first glance.  The *julr*, traditional boulder-homes usually carved from single titanic stones left by the glaciers of *Frest lal Kahbir,* often contain a buried cistern, usually twice the depth of a person with walls of lithified earth, where the residents of the home may submerge themselves.

The magical and technological literacy of the average Tarjali is remarkably high.  General literacy, while a challenge to the peoples of the Coastal Empires, is generally ubiquitous in the Tarjali.  Rich tapestries of historical, artistic, philosophical, and musical traditions contribute heavily to quality of life.  The Tarjali tendency towards spirited debate means any gathering will be a long one, often with the resolution of an argument less important than the exercise itself.  By and large, the Tarjali are not a militant people, and have resisted war and conflict mostly through a combination of geographical isolation, potent magic, and good relations with the rightly feared Sahida.  Spurred by the Sahida, who initially saw a local populace as a method to achieve food security for the increasing numbers of Sahida required to manage increasing tracts of habitable land, the Tarjali have become excellent farmers.  As a result, they lead unconstrained, slow lives, typically seen as lavish and indulgent by neighboring peoples.  The Uqasaq phrase, *"Pijariagiaksaq iqiasu kittuqnarmut tillutittaunngitillutit"*, occasionally used as a rallying phrase for the warhawk factions of the Coastal Empires, means "let the lazy live in caves, let the righteous live in the sun," in reference to the *julr* and the Tarjali way of life.

The appearance of the average Tarjali person is characterized by their rich cultural traditions, environment, and heritage.  Colorful dress, often with complex beadwork representing familial or village heritage, is often accompanied by surprisingly "high-tech" utilitarian gear.  Racial diversity is high within the Tarjali communities, reflecting centuries of migration and trade throughout the *Halligerean* and much older [[The People#The Emeni Remnants|Emeni]] genetic engineering efforts of the local boskers during the *Dawlamanut.*  Religious diversity is somewhat lower than in other regions.  The *Jaks* ascribe this to the lack of serious conflict within Tarjali history and an associated lack of motivation to seek new boons.  The *Katibeh* commonly ascribe this to an efficiency and alignment between the Tarjali way of life and the deities chosen.  Most notably, the Tarjali have the highest concentration of followers of [[The Gods#Mamnue - God of Unknowns|Mamnue]] of any region of Thaja.

### The Coastal Empires  
The Coastal Empires are a group of powerful, mercantile city states along the coast to the south of the Tarjali lands, across the [[The Land#The Region|Ars'Dadshul]].  Unlike the Tarjali, for whom the areas between population centers often possess, at best, an indifferent *aïnhaz,* the lands between the coast and the river Suja have been under permanent cultivation for so long that the threat of attack by monster is rare.  As a result, the cities are much closer to one another than many Tarjali *Calas*, with a dense network of roads and farms between them.  Most cities themselves are surrounded by enormous curtain walls, some even possess huge magical shields, or other ancient defensive technology.  

The Coastal Empires are clustered around the river Suja, and are known for their monumental projects to divert her flow, especially that of her unpredictable spring floods, as far onto the plains as possible.  Efforts to tame the Ars'Dadshul beyond these dikes have been largely in vain as the rolling grasslands don't receive enough rain for effective farming.  The spread of the Empires upriver (and towards the Tarjali) is stalled by two factors.  The first is economic: as Suja turns northward, she steers away from the coast, and any city built along her banks would have no direct access to the sea, no doubt paying enormous tolls to pass her commerce southward.  The second is geographic:  Some hundred miles northward of the northernmost state, the Suja cuts deeply through ridges left by glaciation providing rapids and steep banks which would challenge agrarian colonization.

As a result of this isolation, the Tarjali culture is quite different from that of the Coastal Empires, and although travel between the two (usually along Suja) is common enough, there is no love lost between the two peoples.  All of the city states share a language, Uqasaq, which is a remnant of their time as a unified economic powerhouse region for the Halliger empire. Low Common is spoken by most citizens, while Uqasaq is the language of education, policy, and formal commerce, a structure mirrored by the Tarjali.  Unlike the Tarjali, however, the increasingly dense and stratified population of the Coastal Empires is not uniformly literate.  It is not uncommon to find citizens of any of the states who know no Uqasaq whatsoever, effectively barring them from "polite" society. 

If there is one thing which defines the Coastal Empires, it is wealth.  The fact that Suja's delta has, for millennia, built shallow water to the east of the states rather than at their doorstep means that there is nearly no shelf before the seafloor drops away.  There has never been any serious aquatic kingdom between the Coastal Empires and ships coming from the many islands in the Southern Ocean.  No other regions in the past two eras have moved as much cargo.  Access to Suja also presents a nearly endless quantity of power for manufacturing (when the flood walls hold). 

This wealth and power has been well understood and leveraged by the Coastal Empires for millennia.  It is generally agreed that the downfall of the Halliger Empire began about 900 years ago when the five most powerful city-states on the Suja, all ruled by loosely related Vanara mercantile guilds, negotiated nearly total autonomy within their borders as corporate merchant states.  As leverage, they apologetically claimed that they simply could not continue to meet the mercantile demands of the sprawling Halliger empire and continue to provide the expected cultural institutions and basic conditions to their populous.  The Halliger emperors had long been aware that, a whole world away from Halimarch, their authority was diminished.  The Halliger bureaucracy clicked away, and decided that the material wealth of their homeland was worth the sacrifice of a few poor mill workers on the other side of Thaja.  So was laid the foundation of the modern Coastal Empires.

There are 17 Uqasaq-speaking "empires," several of which are conurbations of cities brought under one rule.  Not all will be addressed in depth here. The five wealthiest (for wealth, not land, determines hierarchies in empires) are, at time of publication, Karno, Kakatoya, Ahom, Oinimal, and Gajapaq.  Karno and Kaktoya are undoubtedly richer than the rest, and represent a consolidation of the Vanara-led coalition which won autonomy from the Halliger.  Oinimal and Gajapaq have essentially filled vacant spots, while Ahom, the northernmost of all 17 empires, has always held its own through a combination of ruthlessness and geographical uniqueness.

There are, broadly, four strata within Uqasaq speaking societies:
1. **Rulers and Owners:** Wealth is status in the Coastal Empires, and the wealthiest can afford indulgences that border on inconceivable.  The common vices of the powerful, such as the desire for extended life or forbidden pleasures are present.  Unique to the empires, however, is a quixotic draw towards displays of philanthropy.  Tupli Mammanannanuq, Ahom's king in the Years 25,128-25,158, famously said, *"Do not suppose you can tempt me, for I have given freely a sum which shames the Sisters."*  This "charity" is usually given to other powerful figures or organizations, who either launder it right back or, more commonly, provide a service that the ruler would consider gauche to pay for themselves.  The [[The People#Fad e Wurds|Fad e Wurds]], in particular, has positioned themselves as worthy recipients of charity, as have several influential religious organizations.

>    Perhaps the damned boskers wouldn't be so put upon if they'd learn, as we did, to live off the fat of the porcine hierarchs who are so desperate to give away their money to make their coffers look unfillably-huge.  At least we actually *do* something with the money, as opposed to the mindless Propriites who would dig their own grave if just to let the shovel "do its purpose."

2. **Clergy**: Power begets structures which sew the seeds of authority to reap control.  Clerical powers have long taken advantage of this effect to provide a "refuge" and comforting structure in return for the *agïs* of a desperate populace.  The Coastal Empires are no exception.  Formal clerical organizations are tolerated only when they benefit the ruling hierarchs.  Those institutions are exalted, provided patronage, monumental architectures, and favorable coverage, while other traditions are viewed as heretical cults, movers-in-shadow, or seditious anti-prosperity radicalists.  This is exemplified best in the church of [[The Gods#Proprius - God of Unbending|Proprius]] which rigidly advocates the status quo in the empires.  Gajapaq doesn't even have a formal law enforcement system, instead relying on the heavily armed Propriites to maintain the order they worship.
3. **Academics:** The presence of the *Silattvik* is curious within the empires.  Ostensibly operating in the service of "innovation," thereby improving the abilities of the mercantile states to turn materials into wealth, the origins of the *Silattvik* lie instead in Halliger influence. To understand why they are still tolerated today, consider the most common fate of a *Silattvik* scholar: traveling elsewhere.  The *Silattvik* operate today less as institutions of true inquiry and more as a way for disillusioned or overly perceptive members of city society to be exempted from the rigors of mercantile work on the grounds of "merit."  Their education is general and the curriculum heavily influenced by the state.  Any scholar of true merit from the *Silattvik* leaves to pursue deeper study elsewhere, or soldiers on in vain as a professor, attempting to drive curiosity into a cohort who simply desires freedom from the mills.  Thus, true "radicals" are motivated to leave peacefully, and the rest are fed propaganda and given a small taste of freedom which will keep them alive until the mills grind them to dust.
4. **Workers:** It is tempting to consider stratifying the workers of the empires into their duties, each conferring slightly different material benefits.  Farmers, millworkers, mechanics, technicians; deeper insight reveals that they are truly one benighted group, kept in constant competition with each other by the higher strata.  Nearly every person from the empires you will meet will be from this strata.  In this strata lies the true power of the empires.  For an example of this, look no further than the recent reorganization of Karno.  By their own admission, they nearly tripled their export productivity by nothing more than rearranging people.  No suite of inventions from the *Silattvik* or chorus of prayers from the clergy has ever come close to this level of impact.  Even the poorest, those who are granted no privilege, serve the city as a threat to keep their brethren in line. 

>    One day the Tarjali might see through their egos enough to witness the deeper truth of their otherwise accurate assessment.  What might millions of people, alike in churning purpose, convey to their surroundings?  One may never know.

The people of the Coastal Empires are, typically, easily recognized by their drab, straight-cut clothing, typically of comparatively high quality.  Even those from comparatively rural regions, farmers, fisherman, riverboaters, live so close to the centers of textile production that their dress is often of finespun material with accents of tight, intricate weave.  Fashions accelerate quickly in the cities, but are typically not enough to make empire dwellers indistinguishable.  Commonly these fashions surround adornments or slight perturbations in the cut or fit of clothes.  The level of religious diversity in the empires is stunning despite the nearly ubiquitous presence of [[The Gods#Proprius - God of Unbending|Proprius]] and [[The Gods#Prunuee|Prunuee]] as deities.  These two clerical doctrines have been locked in a centuries-long cold war which has seen the followers of Prunuee somehow become viewed as "willing to compromise."  Along with the simple weight of people and cultural intermixing from trade, the continuing fallout from this conflict has seen tens, if not hundreds of thousands of people splinter to a great deal of obscure religions.

What follows is a brief elaboration on the wealthiest powers in the empires:

- **Karno**: A conurbation of three cities ruled by a triarchy of Vanara rulers known formally as the Karno Hierarchs, or informally as "The Highest."  Its common name, the City of Three Spires, refers to the massive, cone-shaped skylines, once three hills, that are now ringed by the recently built Karno wall.  Each of the three subordinate cities tries desperately to be the tallest and grandest from afar, with the chambers of each Hierarch traditionally at the highest point of the highest tower.  While all the Coastal Empires claim to have the technology to create *ad'edalires* but to lack sources of *haid* strong enough to power them, only Karno comes close to making this a believable boast, with the technology used to create its amazing *qummuq* kept well under wraps.  In the past hundred years, Karno has achieved a level of production unparalleled in recent history as each of the three subordinate cities has specialized in a different facet of trade, indiscriminately demolishing, rehousing, and constructing and using its chokehold on the world textile trade to prevent anyone from capitalizing on the transition.  Karno is the only city in the empires to be fully enclosed by wall, and it produces almost no staple foods domestically.  With a population well over 1 million, possibly approaching 2 million, it is the biggest, densest city Thaja has seen in 20,000 years.  Most citizens never leave.
- **Kakatoya:** Technically a conurbation of two cities, Kakatoya is defined most by scar left by the second city's destruction 340 years ago.  For a detailed account, see *Kita Hafra Irhueri.*  Briefly, what were once two close, allied Vanara cities came, through a series of complicated political marriages, under the rule of two sisters.  The two famously hated each other. No open conflict arose for nearly 30 years while their mother still lived, although both of their concubine fathers were assassinated.  During the funeral for their mother most of the younger sister's city collapsed into an enormous flesh maw which opened from the bedrock.  The triumphant older sister, Cebu, had sacrificed nearly an entire city to [[The Gods#Shilath Vissar - God of Gnawing|Shilath Vissar]].  Her power consolidated and her authority made absolute by fear, Cebu and Kakatoya were resolute in the face of horror and threats from the other empires.  Had they acted in unison, they almost certainly could have ended Cebu's story immediately.  Instead, blinded by fear and mistrust, none would expose themselves by offering a true alliance necessary to overwhelm Kakatoya's army, and Cebu's gambit succeeded.  Now, the haunted crater that is Kakatoya's fallen sister is used as a breeding and proving ground for uniquely horrific *suliuqanarmik.*  These beasts, touched by the lingering horrors of Shilath Vissar, are such a singularly powerful and desirable asset, they have protected Kakatoya and ushered it into an age of rancid but undeniable prosperity.  Cebu still lives, bouyed beyond her years by unknown magics.  A dedicated group who venerated [[The Gods#Vasu Vasu - God of Passageways|Vasu Vasu]], used by Cebu to dig the tunnels and lay the groundwork for her betrayal, were sacrificed too.  Vasu Vasu is widely regarded to have personally delivered a threat of vengeance to Cebu, the results of which have yet to be seen.
- **Ahom:** A massive, strange city filled with esoteric artifacts, Ahom is the northernmost state and has encompassed the most territory, spreading upward along Suja. Nowhere else are the lost vestiges of prior empires more often seen, as the City of Antiquities sends its agents far and wide.  Ahom is also known for its ruthless trade doctrine.  Nothing passes southward or northward on Suja without inspection and tolling.  Despite Ahom's distance from the sea and its later founding at the end of *Frest lal Kahbir,* its navy is the largest by far and is the *de facto* naval authority of all the empires. While internecine conflict threatens all the cities of the empires, they all faithfully put troops and supplies under Ahom's banner in the defense of all.  Ahom is not an altruistic state, however, and uses its authority well to keep its power secure from strong southern rivals like Karno.  Ahom's position as a destination for antiquities means it is also home to a robust and notoriously excellent hospitality industry which, paradoxically, mostly consists of followers of [[The Gods#Moffel - God of Hospitability|Moffel]].  Ahom's leaders are a council of seven of its most prominent citizens who are appointed and removed, nominally, by a hereditary ruler.
- **Oinimal:** Despite centuries of prominence and prosperity, Oinimal's status as one of the few southern city-states not ruled by Vanara has seen it sidelined in regional politics.  Oinimal is most notable for its semi-aquatic culture and particularly the recent (23 years ago) ascension of an Undine ruler who continues to move public society under, or at least into, the water.  Oinimal is the southern naval anchor of the coastal empires, with two swampier cities south of it remaining nearly irrelevant to the region, and as such maintains a close relationship with Ahom.  It also serves as the main trade hub for purely aquatic races in the area, which also sees it accumulate a number of interesting artifacts.  This close relationship between the two cities contributes to the common Vanara belief that Oinimal is an upjumped underdog who will do anything to get level.  
- **Gajapaq:** The youngest of the empires, Gajapaq is a chaotic place where laws exist only for those who can enforce them, or pay to have them enforced.  Sandwiched between Karno and Kakatoya, Gajapaq was founded initially as a totally autonomous "free-trade zone" during Halliger rule.  It rapidly and predictably spiraled out of Vanara control.  To prevent a diplomatic incident, the surrounding Vanara rulers recognized and legitimized rule of the city by a mercantile gang picked seemingly at random.  The Feathers, now known as The Golden Feathers, were a midsized distribution conglomerate with a corporate hierarchy suddenly thrust into the position of governance.  Buoyed by money and political clout by nearby empires, The Golden Feathers eschewed traditional rule in favor of their core business model.  They contracted law and enforcement to the Propriites, public health to the Temple of Prunuee, education to the Silattvik of Kaktoya, and so on and so forth.  Money is the sole currency of ethics and responsibility, meaning wealthy but unscrupulous conglomerates can make astronomical sums in Gajapaq.  As a city of satellite campuses, however, while uncountable sums flow through Gajapaq, it has almost no regional power beyond that The Golden Feathers can pay for.   
### Steppe Peoples
Bordered on one side by the [[The Land#The Region|Tavilla]] on one side and the [[The Land#The Region|Matalaja]] on the other, the high steppe of the Ars'Stipra is a dry, windswept land.  On the Tavilla side, it is cut by deep and winding gorges as Suja and her tributaries flow downward.  On the Matalaja side, foothills rapidly rise into steep, icepacked peaks.  In the spring, lifegiving rains fall, first along the mountains as warm sea air blows in, and then on the steppe itself.  The Steppe Peoples follow this rain along with enormous herds of wild beasts in a slow but unending migration.  In Spring, they begin in the foothills as rains begin to fall, then through the summer they work towards the Tavilla.  By fall, there is little greenery left, and the people and their animals work their way back up the gorges to the winding, marshy regions around Suja's tributaries, finally wintering in sturdy, permanent structures at the base of the foothills where water is always to be found from seeps or streams.

The Nomads, as the Steppe Peoples are often called, are unique among the current peoples of Thaja.  Bordered by unforgiving geography, the Nomads were barely touched by the Halliger.  They had no mineral wealth like [[The People#Bodil|Bodil]], and no grand reserves of ancient technology like the Sahida.  The animals they herd are not fantastical or particularly valuable.  They had no need of navigational waypoints, no desire for citybuilding or roads, and their land wasn't seen as worth moving to.  On the other side of Thaja from Halimarch, no one could really be bothered to care. It is noted in many texts that the only impact the Halliger really had was the formalization of Jazak, the pidgin language used by the varying nomadic groups.  The Steppe Peoples are sometimes called The Jazak for this reason, although they do not use the phrase themselves.  They also claim no heritage, emerging in groups after *Frest lal Kahbir.*  Before *Frest lal Kahbir,* the region was full of hills and lakes, the population of which largely migrated to the southern coast.  

Far from being valueless savages, the Nomads are a diverse people with strong traditions built upon the new land they walk.  Many have gone on to become renowned boskers, their skills at interpreting *aïnhaz* tuned by necessity.  Where the utilitarian apparatus of the Halliger saw a people as devoid of merit as they were of issues, the Sahida saw a culture of valuable perspective. To this day, Sahida novices are often sent to spend several years with the Nomads, bringing martial training and new schools of thought in return for a place among their surrogate tribe and insights into Thaja and her many moods.  

It is perhaps unsurprising that the Nomads spend a lot of time walking or resting from walking. These times see them filling the hours with conversation, song, discussion, and teaching. In the winters, they lead extremely sedentary, often snowbound lives.  

They view the technology of writing as a tool which negatively impacts memory by offering a crutch, and so they actively work to avoid it.

### The Halliger Remnants
Originator of both Low and High Common.  A true empire, with an emperor and whatnot.  Rose about 4000 years ago, fell about 400 years ago.  It started backsliding about 1000 years back when several city-states, first the coastal empires, negotiated essentially total autonomy within their borders as basically corporate merchant states.  
### Bodil
Originally a Dwarven city some 15,000 years ago, turned into a bomb shelter by the Emeni and then rapidly abandoned when they realized its systems could not support more than were already living there.  Basically everyone fled in panic, those staying being those who believed they could make it for some reason. One of the few places to survive being locked under the ice, Bodil's culture is very odd.  Essentially a homeostatic ecosystem, Bodil's people are masters of ainhaz.  There's even a grove down there!  Upon opening with the fall of the ice age, outsiders were shocked to discover that occupied part of Bodil is dramatically smaller than its full extent and that there remained considerable mineral wealth. ### The Emeni Remnants
The Emeni Conclave was a confederated empire originating in the broad continental shield and mountain ranges of north-central *Coort*.  Landlocked and continually mired in a series of small-scale conflicts, the peoples that would become the Emeni were largely ignored by the great powers of the Dawlamanut as a backwater with nothing beyond mineral wealth.  After it became clear that the impact of The Great Bargain would be a global ice age, the Conclave was formed as the doomed nations of *Coort* realized they had to act together if some stability was to be assured.  Uniquely in the history of Thaja, the Conclave, named after [[The People#The Emeni Three|The Emeni Three]], were founded with a specifically fatalistic outlook.  Many of their genuinely impressive technical accomplishments were in the service of trying to keep at least *someone* alive through the ice age.  

They offered their help to anyone who would take it, resorting to military force only to resist a few notable incursions by other desperate powers.  In these wars, their fatalism proved an unexpected asset, as many of the most devastating and horrific weapons of the *Dawlamanut* were deployed against the Emeni.  Refusing to be cowed, the Emeni realized the aggressors didn't have the ability to fight protracted wars.  One after another, attacking forces met the Emeni on the field of battle, deployed a weapon designed to immediately end a war, and then were forced to swallow their pride and join the Emeni effort as the next ranks of Emeni troops, nearly all of whom were volunteers, climbed over the bodies of their fallen brethren and offered first to help someone, anyone, see a future.

The Emeni's most successful project is, undoubtedly, the [[The People#The Sahida|Sahida]] order who preserved much of their wisdom through *Frest lal Kahbir.*  The Sahida emerged into a warming world with the strength of spirit to see the greatest tenets of the Emeni Conclave preserved.  The fatalism of the Emeni had been replaced by a stoic and unquenchable vision which persists to this day.

The Emeni Conclave fell roughly in the Year 14,450.  It had always been understood that it could never last, but as the glaciers and snow buried enormous swaths of land, the Emeni's legendary fatalism failed them.  The few remaining factions descended into petty conflicts, wasting what little resources they had trying to steal scraps from the others.  Ironically, it was the Sahida who rang the final bell on the Emeni Conclave.  Barely 1,000 years old, the Sahida order watched the Emeni leadership devolve and fortified the [[The Land#The Region|Tavilla]].  As the hammer of the glaciers pushed the ragged and squabbling Emeni south, they were driven against the anvil of the Sahida who offered them the same customary grace of the Emeni past.  Few were wise enough to take it.  By 14,445, the Sahida departed their fortresses on the Tavilla as the glaciers ground them to dust, and by 14,450 the last communication from Emeni territory was received.  

To be Emeni and still live now is to carry, at the least, the legacy and memory of a golden age 14,000 years past. Whatever this means to an Emeni, they are defined by a shared experience: to be Emeni is to carry a Secret.  Those that still live have, necessarily, found some way to unnaturally long life, of which there are varying potencies. Some of them shared those secrets with others, in an effort to preserve the wisdoms of the Emeni for all.  These altruistic Emeni rarely survived to the present day, dying in *Frest lal Kahbir* to aid another.  Many of the remaining Emeni, then carry a true Secret, of which they are the only master.

The Emeni are a dying breed, surviving into this future only by the technological graces of the *Dawlamanut* and whatever valor or folly kept them alive during *Frest lal Kahbir.*  They are anyone; indistinguishable from their culture unless they make themselves so.  While the stereotypical Emeni is a techno-hermit, fortified in a remote tower which annihilates any who come too close, the reality is much different.  Every Emeni who is still alive has a *reason*, some driving force which leads them to maintain their life against increasingly long odds.  Some have begun nomadic quests to see the whole of Thaja as it changes.  Some hold shadowy positions as advisors to powerful leaders.  It is even rumored that Emeni remain in some numbers within the Sahida.

One unifying factor between the Remnants is their ability with technology or magic.  Luck, as it were, ran out for the Emeni thousands of years ago.  Those that remain do so by prowess alone.  As such, their ability to interface with ancient systems, wield ancient weapons, and perform esoteric acts of technomancy or medicine, is generally the most telling way to distinguish them.

#### Beamhalders
The Beamhalders were a group of seven, and then eventually five, heroes who nearly singlehandedly saved the system of *oerleverbosks* from reaching a bottleneck point.  After 4,000 years of ice, the bosks had suffered blow after blow and, according to some estimates, as few as three remained that had both viable saplings and complete archives, all centered in equatorial regions.  The [[The People#The Emeni Remnants|Emeni]] effort to forestall this issue by leveraging the dwindling resources of the *Dawlamanut* to grow heartier bosk trees as a resolute failure.  In a world of crumbling institutions, icebound lands, and desperate people, it was becoming harder to protect, let alone justify, the bosks.  Two Sahida, the philosopher Ilya Kalm Uka (*trueblood* daughter of Tan Kalm Uka) and the warrior Wren Terhen Solipi, and two boskers, the elder Hagscein Man and an ambitious grovekeeper known as Popularo, formed a band whose sole goal was to rally the remaining powers to the preservation of the tenuous link to the past.

Along a perilous journey from the northernmost remaining bosk to Halimarch, one of the few remaining city-states, all four were killed in a fight with a Zelekhut Inevitable which surprised them and attacked without reason.  All four were revived by Pthera Dun, a cleric of Fend who claimed to have been teleported to the battle by a force outside her control, and then the group was five.  

Upon reaching Halimarch, the five were successful in convincing the rulers of the state and nearby fortified towns to aid in maintaining, at least, a clear corridor to their bosker neighbors.  Halimarch's tetrarchy viewed the project as a way to project Halimarch's status as a remnant of a golden age past and assigned Tallus the Spandarion, a Hallimarkian wizard of much renown, to the project.  Tallus was, according to Dun, a "well read but poorly traveled".  In Wren Solipi's less charitable approximation, he had "gained enormous power at the cost of his ability to tolerate the least amount of walking."  

The six succeeded first in establishing a corridor to the northern bosk, finding the Inevitable they had faced prior dead and its wings, powerful magical components, gone.  They then brought proof of their accomplishment, including duplicate *rekordplaats* first to Halimarch, and then on a journey of thousands of miles across the equatorial regions to stir support for the defense of the bosks.  Nearly on the other side of Thaja, in what are now the Coastal Empires, the six were approached by a *tri vang* familiar (an unusual tropical bird with red and yellow plumage, akin to a large pheasant) which relayed that, out on the snowy wastes of what would become the [[The Land#The Region|Ars'Dadshul]], its master had found that a *Grut Oerleverbosk* at the foot of the [[The Land#The Region|Tavilla]] which could be recovered.  Intrigued, the six met a hooded traveler along the wastes.  Popularo, as the least martial of the party, provides an interesting insight into what transpired in his account (translated into Low Common near the beginning of the *Halligerian*):

*"As we trudged across endless plains of windblown snow, we had little to keep us company but a vague sense of unease to keep us company.  After four hours, we saw through the blown snow a figure, standing motionless, hood up but arms curiously bare.  Immediately on guard, we approached slowly, all of realizing in our own time that the glare of the sun somehow became brighter and brighter.  There was a warmth on the skin which could be felt through the clothes, and the sluggishness of a nap in dappled sunshine after midday, and then it was everywhere.  A blinding brightness, no longer warm but overwhelmingly white, more luminous than the snow.  I could see my bones through the orange translucency of my skin, and then she spoke:*

*"Thank you for coming," the voice said, her voice thin and sad, but all penetrating. "Forgive me, but I must test you against the coming horrors.  Here in this cradle of plasma, no harm will come to you, but you must fight like your life depends on it."*

*I had no idea what she meant, but as she finished, I felt released, as if I'd been held transfixed all my life and yet not known.  As always, Terhen acted first, releasing without hesitation the most devastating of energies from her saj.  The blast dissipated in the brightness before it even reached the figure, who sent a questing tendril of energy, nearly invisible against the background radiance, which caught Terhen as if by accident, cleanly burning her in half.*

*"The mahatalli is well known to me," the voice said, "and would protect you well anywhere but here.  You must learn to fight without it"*

*Inured now to sudden violence, the rest of us lept to action.  Tallus had an idea clearly, and began some intricate conjuration, covered by the actions of myself and Kalm.  I fell to nothing more than a glance, burning eyes which filled me with such sorrow as I had never known, lifetimes upon lifetimes of untouched pain, the sense of seeing joy from afar, and a desire to vanish into an intricate, soothing blackness.  As I lay down, I saw two things which surprised me.  Hagescein sat, skin smoldering and clothes reduced to ash, a look of wonder on his face, and Dun was laughing.  She was untouched by it all, and her glittering laughter was somehow brighter than all around us.*

*The last I saw was Tallus, a look of confident derision on his face, unleash a masterstroke spell, which dissolved in the air around him as the glowing essence stripped his skin to the bone.  It was the first time I'd seen Tallus afraid.*"
Popularo, 18,390

Accounts of this event exist from four of the seven, with the exceptions of Dun, Hagescein, and Razza Mnesch, the last known warlock of Part, and the orchestrator of the *Luminant Teacher*, a spell deployed here for the first time, which channels an enormous amount of *haid* to contain a battleground for training of powerful magics.  What is clear from all the accounts is that only Dun survived, and that, while the annihilation of the party took mere minutes, the *Teacher* lasted for hours.  Dun admitted to having a conversation with Razza, but never revealed the details.

With Razza's test apparently passed, she joined the seven, and led them to the *Grut Bosk* which remains to this day in the center of [[The Land#Topaz|Topaz]].  The bosk was miraculously intact, its keepers alive, but enthralled by a psychic monster of terrible power.  Before the fight, Razza explained that the monster had been brought to the plane by her hand, an action which had warranted the attention of an Inevitable she had killed.  With a weapon forged from the Inevitable's body, she could kill it, but she could not fight the hordes of enthralled monsters. In a battle that took nearly two hours, Ilya Uka was grievously wounded by a terrifying Hollow Serpent raised by the monster in the grove.  Dun was forced to choose: save Ilya Uka at the cost of her own life, or leave the Sahida to her wounds.  The decision was made for her as Hagscein stepped forward and allowed the Serpent to strike at him.  Wren Solipi's account describes the event as follows:

*"After Razza humbled all of us but Dun out on the wastes, Hagscein had been different.  The old man was light of step and quick with a joke.  He and Dun would sometimes share a joke which made no sense to the rest of us.  Kalm told me later, from within that stone, that she now understands why, but couldn't explain.  Hagscein's understanding of [[The Magic#Aïnhaz|aïnhaz]] was always nearly incomprehensible to the rest of us, he could feel things in the air that happened hundreds of miles away. For those that don't believe me, I once saw him sit in the rain and stay dry.*

*When Kalm was struck down, I cried out, but my cry was deafened by a quiet whisper from Razza, that thin voice filling every piece of us.  "It must be now," she said, and she sounded afraid.  Hagescein laughed then, and strode out from behind my terrified guard like a young brawler, beating his chest and hurling curses.  The thing lashed its barbed tail and it punched through his chest but somehow, when it pulled, he stood firm.  I watched the gooseflesh rise on his skin, and through the blood burbling from his mouth he just kept laughing.  He set his feet and reality pulled in towards him, an effect so disquieting even the beasts slavering at my saj went still.  Above us, the sun suddenly burned through the clouds and the trees shone with a radiant green.  "Be free with me, brother," he said.  For a fraction of a second, everything bent towards him and then with a gentle outward breeze like a sigh, Hagescein and the serpent were gone and a tranquil ripple passed through the very fabric of my being.  Dun's laugher was bright and I saw her dancing Kalm's spirit back into her body.  A second later there was an ear splitting detonation and a pillar of blinding light, and the oppressive presence of the beast at the center of the grove dissipated.*"
Wren Terhen Solipi, 18,390

Within the restored Grut Bosk, enough keepers had survived the battle to hold out, and the records and knowledge within were such a boon to the remaining peoples of Thaja that the future of the extant bosks was assured.  Halimarch positioned itself well as a center of this knowledge, setting the grounds for the Halliger Empire, a future which Dun, Wren Solipi and, surprisingly, Tallus all spoke out against.  

Razza was nowhere to be found for days, but her familiar assured the remaining party that she would be back.  Under the direction of Wren Solipi, Ilya Uka was brought to a nearby Sahida stronghold and was survived by the timely action of the creation of the first [[The People#The Sahida|Fumukaxi]]. When Razza returned, she explained she had offered her weapon to Part to be granted to Part's next warlock.  

Following the events at the Grut Bosk, the five remaining members of the party, guided occasionally by Ilya Uka in her stasis, embarked on a number of deep explorations into glaciated wilds to reestablish groves, or simply recover their records where reestablishment was impossible, a series of quests which took them nearly three centuries.   The Beamhalders, known in their time by multiple names across Thaja, were keen to avoid fame and accolades from all but a select few.  Even Popularo, who had grand designs to see a Thaja reborn around the gentle governance of the groves, was the last to publish an account of the journey, and did so only as part of an effort to encourage the incorporation of important narratives into bosk records.  The Beamhalders are a strange group to observe with the benefit of retrospect, as their desire for limited notoriety was consistently stymied by their key role in preserving the very history they were written into.

Their longstanding cooperation was brought to an end when Wren Solipi was killed in 18,705.  For nearly a decade, her fellow Beamhalders had attempted to convince her to retire, as her age was beginning to show.  Popularo, likewise getting quite old after a lifetime spent mostly outside the soothing magics of the groves, saw the writing on the wall and left the group shortly afterward, retiring to a nearby bosk.  Dun, heartbroken by the preventable death of Wren Solipi, retired to a productive, and by her account, fulfilling life of teaching, after turning down leadership offers from both the Sahida and the precursor to the Fad e Bosks.  Dun died in 19,374, prophesizing (correctly) that the *Frest lal Kahbir* was near its end.  

Tallus, the only human member of the party, had been kept alive well past his years by a combination of Dun's efforts, Bosker magic, and important artifacts recovered throughout the party's adventures.  When the party split, it was assumed he'd take up teaching for the rest of his days.  Instead, he disappeared from record for nearly 200 years, emerging miraculously youthful and with a series of proposed magical reforms to help his home city of Halimarch continue to consolidate regional power.  His mercurial and vacillating personality is the subject of several books, all of them ending with a cautionary word on the note of his death about those who would idolize him.  

Razza, who never committed to record more than simple and pragmatic suggestions, had been occasionally seen during Tallus's absence.  Shortly after Tallus's public reappearance, however, he was killed in a remarkably swift battle with none other than Razza, along with a Marut Inevitable.  Upon the wizard's death, the Inevitable translocated off the plane and Razza was immediately detained and sentenced to death by the Halimarch tetrarchy.  Days later, in advance of a trial, Dun arrived in Halimarch to advocate for her old friend.  Dun, as a renowned hero, was granted access to Razza's cell, where Razza apologized to Dun for killing Tallus, explaining it was part of her penance to Axiomite rulers for prior transgressions.  Dun left smiling and laughing, and boarded her ship for the return journey.  The next day at noon, hours before her scheduled trial, Razza simply melted the warded walls of her cell into slag and walked out of the city, never to be seen again.
### Halligerian (20,389 to 25,011)
#### Nondo the Exile
Self styled as "The Last Dwarf of Bodil", Nomp Dorcha (shortened to the mononym "Nondo") was exiled from [[The People#Bodil|Bodil]] during the closing years of the [[The World#Halligerian (20,389 to 25,011)|Halligerian]] for attempting to begin a popular revolt against the extant order of Bodil which he viewed as isolationist.  Having traveled to the Coastal Empires in his youth, he was taken by the extravagant lifestyles of the city elite. His mostly Dwarven popular revolt was swiftly quelled when Bodil's leadership encouraged the Dwarven youths to venture into the dark and seize the riches they desired, wherein they were swiftly massacred by monsters.  

Nondo survived, and though he was not the last dwarf in Bodil, he was the last of his posse.  In a mixture of shame and fear, he fled Bodil and somehow survived a perilous journey across the Matalaja mountains and Ars'Stripra, reemerging into recorded history in and amongst the Tarjali.  He lived a complicated but well-storied life, emerging in some stories as a folk hero fending off roving monsters, and in others a petty thief repeatedly put to justice by the Sahida.  In 24,872 he arrived in Topaz, where he drowned in a *Maqubahrya* and was not found until well after he could have been revived.
