## Thaja
It is the year 25,447.  This world is Thaja, the lifegiver and home of the earthbound races, jewel of the material plane, and cradle of *[[Stories/Pre-Joman Tabletop Setting/Chapters/Magic#Haid and Agïs|agïs]].*  She and her sisters dance eternally with *Part*, the star that feeds the world.  Since the destruction of her nearest sister *Vilfye* 23,000 years ago, the four sisters Thaja, *Prunuee*, *Balk*, and *Gzethye* mourn, their enormous *agïs* quiet, the skies dark. Travel to and from the sisters is well recorded until 19,200 years ago, when the Mazithran Gates fell at the onset of the *Ivicstoarm*.   It is said that, in ancient times, it was not uncommon to find avatars of all the sisters on the surface of Thaja and *Vilfye*.  Now, only *Prunuee* braves the storm to manifest on Thaja.

Many people have lived, died, ascended, and fallen again upon the stone and under the seas of Thaja.  Empires have risen and fractured  Fools and sages have grown to threaten the stars, raised great structures, then returned to caves to humble themselves.  Gods have died upon her and will do so again.  Thaja shines because of the life upon her, which constantly forges an incomprehensible and ever-shifting network of *agïs*, bursting the banks of comprehension in a shower of colors, sounds, dreams, and joys.  All of that life, however, nearly always sits in the shadow of its own complexities.  It is the rare Thajan person or people who transcend their station to care, truly, about the world beyond her skies.  This is no failing, simply a consequence of the need of all beings to eat, drink, find compansionship, and etch their creativity into a medium they might mold.

It is for this reason that the history of Thaja, and all of us, is dominated by empires.  The biggest of them have brought the whole of Thaja under one banner, sent all her many peoples on errands of their own devising.  It has never lasted.  When these empires fall, fade, or willingly split, it is simply entropy returning the world to equilibrium.  As the great [[The People#The Sahida|Sahida]] philosopher Tan Kalm Uka observed, "those who wish to run must first learn to lay upon the ground in stillness."  The testament to this philosophy is reflected in the 12,000 year history of the Sahida themselves.

The last of these empires to have left us all in its shadow is that of the [[The People#The Halliger Remnants|Halliger]].  The Halliger emperors and their enormous state apparatus came to claim domain over all of Thaja's humanoid races. One of the Halliger Empire's biggest goals, now its biggest mark on the world, was a consistent, centuries long effort to standardize terminology, technology, history, and practices worldwide. Not only an intellectual effort, centuries of massive effort raised the *Weistiens* to aid in navigation, and engaged in targeted cultivation of the lands around *thorofaars* to connect disparate populations through hostile lands.

The Halliger Geography: as Thaja is roughly a sphere, the Halliger saw fit to divide her into four equal sections (sektors), each a triangular projection onto her surface.  Those inclined to geometry may find an intriguing discussion into the context of this cartographic decision in the introduction to the *Kita Buldam Mukthasa.* The four *sektors* have four vertices (*hoekpunt* in High Common), one of which, the *Priemhoekpunt* or Prime Vertex is centered on Halimarch.  From there, one layline travels due north, bisecting Thaja's icebound pole, while the other two travel mostly through deep water, tracing out the grand Southern Ocean.  On the "back" of the globe lies the fourth *sektor,* dominated by the continent [[The Land#Coort|Coort]].  The lands of the Tarjali, the [[The People#The Coastal Empires|Coastal Empires]], and the [[The People#Steppe Peoples|Jazak speaking tribes]] encompass a roughly triangular southern peninsula of *Coort.*  

The Halliger calendar: the 480 day year is broken into 10 *spokes* of 48 days, split into 8, 6-day *tines.* Each day is split into 20 *twirls* (hours).   This calendar is standardized to be as accurate to the orbital period as possible.  The orbital period was precisely engineered 17,000 years ago to make calendrical matters easier.  That it also helped the engineers win a key battle is not commonly remarked upon in histories.
## A History
What follows is the briefest of surveys of more than 20,000 years of history.  
### Antiquity (before Year 0)
Much of the established history before Year 0 falls into two categories: archaeology and interpretation.  For an in-depth discussion of archaeological finds and an examination of pre-history, the authors recommend The Azmasad compiled by the 12th Nikab of Topaz or any one of its condensed primers.  Briefly, scholars agree that the roughly 25,000 years of recorded history represent roughly two thirds of "civilized" history.  While significant debate exists over the extent and accomplishments of civilizations pre-recording, the usual reticence of the gods in the matter of history has been worn down by millennia of questing scholars, and some accurate dates can be established.  It is known that the first humanoid beings to walk Thaja did so nearly 400,000 years ago.  Only a handful of remnants of these peoples are known.   It is also known that a massive glaciation event killed nearly all surface life roughly 130,000 years later and that no being walked Thaja's icy surface for another 150,000 years.  Rather suddenly, it appears, there was a proliferation of surface life and a receding glacial line.  From this point humanoids of varying kinds slowly developed magical and technological skills.

It is worth briefly addressing interpretation.  In many respects, it is rarely the place of the scholar to say with certainty, "it was thus at that time."  Every historian has, at one point, simply wished for one of the gods to describe the lore and histories in depth.  As the saying goes (transliterated from Tarjali), *"Why does [[The Land#The Region|Suja]] not flow straight to sea?"*  The answer, of course, is immaterial.  Rivers do not flow in straight lines because it is not the nature of the material (or, indeed, immaterial) world to follow ordered laws in all things.  Why do the gods not seek to empower some chosen faction with all knowledge?  Because it is not the nature of the world to allow unchecked powers to grow.  To do so would be akin to climbing out of one of Suja's many canyons.  Each step away from her bank is exponentially steeper, you gain less ground and expend more effort.  To understand this, one requires philosophy twofold more than inquiry.  

[[The People#Baston Pawkat|Baston Pawkat]], a warlock of Part who was already well known by Year 0, was requested by a powerful ruler or nation to learn the age of Part itself.  The origin of the request is lost, but what is well preserved is Pawkat's reply, apparently a direct interpretation of Part's intention: *"Count the smallest moments from the first time someone imagined the possibility of you to the last time it was plausible for someone to remember the idea of you.  I have seen that many turns."*  The Sahida suggest that the impossibility of the task and the incomprehensibility of the answer are the truest answer possible.  Put differently, it is not the truth that matters, but the relationship between that truth and yours.  As one interprets history, it is quite often the case that no "truth" can be found, but that the distance between one experience and another can be approximated, and meaning derived from this measurement.
### Ars'Dalan (Year 0 to 19,200)
The *Ars'Dalan*, literally the "Years of the Highest", is widely considered the time in recorded history wherein the denizens of Thaja had the greatest agency and magical prowess.  Although myths of the time suggest a world wherein avatars of the gods walked constantly upon the earth and anyone could have their heart's desire without so much as a blink, the few surviving writings from the time indicate it was a time of of particularly great divide.  While powerful wizards raised the Mazithran Gates and began trade and exchange in earnest with *Balk* and *Vilfye*, even walking amongst the glittering crystal clouds of *Prunuee*, whole continents had fallen to some earlier cataclysm and were peopled by scared bands of remnants and refugees.  An enormous and brutal war was fought between the [[The People#Hawi|Hawi]] and at least one surface power which is known to have permanently obliterated an entire chain of islands.  

Perhaps most telling is the way in which the history of the *Ars'Dalan* is recorded--nearly the same way it is today.  Thaja's peoples were under no presupposition that they were at the apogee of culture, and it remains hotly debated if the excesses of the time were worth the catastrophes.
#### 25,000 years ago (Year 0)
The establishment of the *oerleverbosks* (in a much different form) begins here, with the bosks themselves forming the only continuous historical record to have survived to present day.  *Rekordplaats* of the time were smaller, typically only 100-150 years, but with very large rings for ease of recording.  The tree responsible is presumed extinct.  There are 13 *rekordplaats* dated earlier, although the date recording system of the time was self-referential, and without a continuous reference, exact dates are unknown.  It is presumed that the oldest, however, is less than 700 years older than the *First Rekordplaat*.  The current year, 25,447, reflects the first contiguous year of record.
#### 23,000 years ago (Year 2,000)
*Vilfye* is destroyed in a poorly understood cataclysm. The Sisters themselves fall into disarray, with freak weather, seismic phenomenon, apparitions, upswells of monsters, and gargantuan extraplanar rifts spewing forth horrors.  As the embattled peoples of The Sisters desperately attempt to keep the Mazithran Gates open, it becomes rapidly clear to the established powers that, without intervention, chunks of the shattered sister will rain down upon them all.  This event, it is determined, will be the end of nearly everyone.  So begins a herculean effort on the two remaining populated sisters to avert this disaster.  While many thousands of heroes contributed, best remembered are [[The People#The Emeni Three|The Emeni Three]], who braved the terrors of the fractured *Vilfye* to break apart a chunk too large to be deflected or destroyed any other way.  As observers on the ground watched the piece of the shattered Sister explode, a simultaneous triple-banded aurora was seen in the atmosphere of Prunuee, assumed to be the souls of the Three returning to the Guardian Sister to continue serving the people of the system.
#### 20,000 years ago (year 5,000)
From roughly 5,240-5,270, the Hawi and a surface power known as Caliban fought a savage and genocidal war over a chain of volcanic islands the Calibanites called home.  Facts are sparse, but it appears that both sides believed the enormous power of the vulcanism could be harnessed as a weapon.  How this belief originated is unknown.  Perhaps it was a terrible misunderstanding of [[Stories/Pre-Joman Tabletop Setting/Chapters/Magic#Aïnhaz|aïnhaz]], or more likely it was a case of posturing and propagandizing which spiraled out of control. In any case, the war ended with the Caliban islands wiped from Thaja's face and dead Hawi citizens washing up on every shore for months.

>Once again the Tarjali obsession with the isolation of *serij* as merely a magical curiosity shames the histories, the greatest wizards, and Thaja herself.  When some senile, thousand-year old Bosker crone draws the wrath of the heavens down to smite a poaching woodsman, it's common magic, but imagine that the greatest of our history's sorcerers could weaponize a mountain full of Thaja's lifeblood?  Mark that as heresy, of course, lest some ambitious *jak* try to build a house without quarrying stone.  Why protect the populace with lies?  Why convince the most promising wizards of their own ineptitude? 

In another poorly understood calamity, the *Ivicstoarm* descends upon Part and the Sisters in the Year 5,800, shattering the Mazithran Gates and, as far as anyone knows, permanently ending physical and extraplanar travel between the Sisters.  The storm appeared with little warning, and despite desperate efforts, quickly overwhelmed the keepers of the Gates, causing enormous magical disturbances which kill tens of thousands.  At the time, the Temple of Part was an organized religion present in most established surface settlements.  At the onset of the storm, the highest ranking official in this temple made a short, cryptic exposition (of which no direct recording exists) before being incinerated on the spot with the rest of their clergy.  Since then there has been no established clergy of Part.
### Dawlamanut (Year 5,800 to 12,641)
The *Dawlamanut*, literally the "Era of Empires", was an era characterized by the rise and fall of enormous powers.  With Thaja cut off due to the *Ivicstoarm*, Vilfye destroyed, and most of the horrors from the worst days of the *Ars'Dalan* vanquished, the great powers of Thaja had little to do but expand and war with each other.  The *Dawlamanut* was also a period of rapid technological advancement.  With those powers who had been reliant on the Sisters hamstrung by the death of *Vilfye* and the *Ivicstoarm*, and the large mercantile wizarding powers of the Mazithran Gates either destroyed or rendered useless, magic use saw a regression amongst the surface powers, while a renaissance period of technological developments drove the construction of powerful empires.  Beneath the Thajan seas, however, a battered and jaded Hawi sought a magical masterstroke.
#### 18,000 years ago (Year 7,000)
Orbital period is precisely adjusted to 480, 20-hour days.  Thaja's rotation is also slowed very slightly.  As a consequence, Thaja's natural precession ceases, entombing the northern pole in ice.  Accomplished by the leadership of an equatorial power known as *Yk Mi Pel Wo,* whose traditionally mellow populace were sold on the action as an act of great philanthropy, the enormous consequence of this action would take several years to fully manifest.  The immediate effect, however, was the demoralization of a northerly power (the *Zentu*) who realized right away that they were doomed.  The majority of the *Zentu* army, which was a small but potent force of largely psychic spellcasters, was overwhelmed by the grief of their people and fell to a counterattack by besieged *Yk Mi Pel Wo* soldiers who almost certainly would not have carried the day otherwise.  The battle was ultimately inconsequential, as the doomed Zentu could not have truly threatened *Yk Mi Pel Wo,* and the enraged [[The People#Hawi|Hawi]] overwhelmed the jubilant victors, dragging them and their entire landmass to the abyss as they'd done with Caliban just 2,000 years prior.  It is generally agreed that The Great Bargain was, at least in part, enabled by the skills of the captured *Yk Mi Pel Wo.*
#### 13,000 years ago (Year 12,000)
The Great Bargain - the Hawi change the topology of the seafloor, shallow coastal waters and steep drops to abyss, very little else.  Changes slowly trigger an ice age.

#### 12,000 years ago (Year 13,000)
The Sahida order is founded as a response to the changing conditions on the surface of Thaja as the [[The People#The Emeni Remnants|Emeni Conclave]] realized it was simply too big to survive in the coming resource constrained situation.  The ice age begins, known (in Tarjali) as *Frest lal Kahbir*, literally "The Period of Great Hostility" not just for the climate, but the response of the ainhaz of most regions becoming decidedly more hostile, a condition which persists to this day.
#### 7,000 years ago (Year 18,000)
The number of stable *Bosks* drops precipitously low despite the best efforts of a variety of actors.  Seven legendary heroes, two boskers, a cleric, two Sahida, a wizard, and the last known warlock of Part, go on a centuries long quest to gather support to keep the remaining *bosks* in tact. On the way, one bosker is killed, and one Sahida wounded so grievously she is rendered nearly insensate and becomes the first [[The People#The Sahida|Fumukaxi]].  From then on the remaining five heroes, known in [[The People#The Halliger Remnants|High Common]] as the *[[The People#Beamhalders|Beamhalders]]* embarked on a quest to recover *rekordplaat* archives from *bosks* overrun by monsters, buried in ice, or otherwise lost.  To see a true testament to their success, look no further than the presence of this chapter.  The *Beamhalders* were seen as founding heroes by the Halliger despite several of them explicitly stating their wishes to see the mistakes of the *Dawlamanut* never repeated.
