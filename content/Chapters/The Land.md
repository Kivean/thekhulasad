## Thajan Geography
The vast majority of Thajan landmasses are chains of large islands connected by regions of shallow water.  Since The Great Bargain 13,000 years ago, the term "ocean" is used only to refer to areas of deep water, while "sea" refers to shallow water areas.  The assumption of most of the humanoid races is that nearly any area of deep water is patrolled by endless hordes of [[The People#Hawi|Hawi]] monstrosities, but the situation under the water appears to be much the same as on land.  Population centers, where they exist, are sparse, separated by inhospitable or outright hostile regions.  In the 3-dimensional world under the water, there is even less sense in when and where "people" are.  The [[The People#The Tarjal|Tarjali]] have no Nikab dedicated to study of underwater geography.

>And they never will while they stand by their assumption that all that matters exists within spitting distance of their Calas.  For endless fun, I recommend the approach relayed to me by my mentor: ask a Tarjali scholar where the most important place in the world is.  No matter their reply, and especially if their answer is "there is none," insist that such a place must be under the ocean, and watch the mental gymnastics they perform under their pert little hats to justify why that answer must certainly be wrong.

## The Region

The region the story takes place in is a vaguely crescent shaped stretch of foothills and badlands bordering the edge of a large, raised continental landmass, commonly called the Tavilla.  Off the mesa formerly flowed a river, Suja, not large usually but subject to seasonal floods.  The badlander's theft of rain killed the river, and with it the various communities and ecological niches along its path.  To the south is a large expanse of desert, which then reaches a sea.  Great nations once rimmed the coast, before succumbing to excess and environmental pressures from the badlander's theft of rain.  To the north, the arid mesa eventually becomes high steppe before a colossal mountain chain rises.  There are known cultures beyond the mountains, but much like the eastern Himalayas, passes which one could move an army through are rare, and the motivation is slim.  On the north side of the mountains, (and their southern slope), are rich forests.   

Mesa: Tavilla (Uqasaq: Aviaru runnairmut, lit: "*barrier to entry*", after the longstanding rivalry between the steppe peoples and the Tarjali.  The name was coined before it was understood that the Tarjal and the steppe peoples were not one in the same. Jazak: Stovla)

Steppeland: Ars'Stipra (Uqasaq: Paalaad, lit: "*bad land*", Jazak: Rovlan, lit: "*homeland*")

## Topaz
Topaz sits right where Suja exits the Tavilla, and the site is predated by the Grut Bosk.

Topaz is the former capital city of the Badlands.  A technological powerhouse, it was the original colleges of Topaz who orchestrated the Wailing Canyons, or so the story is told. In reality, it is tough to imagine that such a city would have been born where it was, away from the river Suja.  Whether the canyons predate Topaz or vice versa is a matter lost to time.

The great theft of rain was a wonder of technological and ecological planning, but it didn't win Topaz many friends.  Small communities along Suja's banks or estuaries soon found their only source of water drying up.  The mighty Coastal Empires who flourished in Suja's delta fared no better.  While the process undoubtedly must have taken years, many histories describe it as a process quick enough to elicit confusion and fear among these communities.  Most likely, by the time they realized Topaz was responsible, it was too late.  By this time, most of the small communities had already died, their people assimilating into the cultures of the Badlanders or the Coastal Empires.  

In the oral histories of the Badlanders, the Coastal Empires led a great campaign across the desert which stalled and died from lack of water.  This obscures the truth of the matter, which is that there actually were significant border skirmishes and wars, with sections of the wailing canyons destroyed, calas brought down, and thousands of Badlanders forced into hiding or slavery.  Topaz, however, never fell.  The titanic crawling warmachines of the Coastal Empires were stout enough to make the desert crossing, but they simply did not have the resources to lay siege to Topaz.  In the end, the very esotericism of the Wailing Canyons, which now plagues the remaining Badlanders, saved Topaz.  No Coastal warband ever managed to establish a reliable outpost with its own water, even when they managed to climb onto the Mesa.  The water hungry rock simply made collection impossible, and drilling operations were constantly hounded by desperate Badlander guerrillas.  Even the most bloodthirsty imperial commanders were eventually forced to ditch their positions and return across the desert.  For a few years, there was peace.

The Coastal Empires did indeed send a last-ditch effort when the situation became dire enough.  Alliances were forged which would have been unthinkable.  A force was developed which almost certainly could have overrun Topaz without a siege, but by then it was *much* too late.  Topaz had gained enough secrets from Mamnue to render the assembled sand-fleet a non-issue, through means which have stayed a secret ever since.  Somewhere in the desert, perhaps under thousands of tons of sand, lies the largest land army ever seen in Joman's world.

Topaz might have secured its place in history for time immemorial, but greed is an insidious disease.  The pragmatic, if selfish, leaders who oversaw the Canyon projects, the drying of Suja, and the defense of the Canyons, were eventually replaced by unseasoned scholars and scientists.  While the dominant philosophy was one of satiation, pleased to innovate in the safety earned by their forefathers, some minds had ambitions beyond.  After all, they had defeated the coastal empires, and the rim of Calas at the northern edge of the mesa had never been breached by raiders. Indeed, at that time, the raiders had no organization, steppe nomads had no interest in attacking the heavily defended Calas, and the empires beyond the mountains had barely made contact.

It was these ambitions that drove both the Golden Age of Topaz *and* The Fall.

Following The Fall, Topaz has become a dangerous and uncontrolled city run amok with magical haywire and technological mayhem.  Intense superstition among the Badlanders, which, to be fair, is mostly based in reality, keeps most outsiders away from Topaz.  The Remnants, as they are, also rarely travel into Topaz. What knowledge could be salvaged has already mostly been salvaged.  The rest is either shrouded by the influence of Mamnue or destroyed.  Strange sounds and lights can be seen and heard from the city, odd smells drift on the wind, and strange and dangerous beasts sometimes slip outwards from cracks in the ancient shieldwall.  

Perhaps most telling of Topaz's current condition is the attitude of the Arbiter, the central cogitator of the Wailing Canyons, towards the city which once commanded it.  Following the fall, it severed command links with the city and treats all sensor data from within and near the city as false. In an effort to protect itself, it shunned the Agïs of its Topaz arms. Curiously, it still sends an ever-varying allotment of water into the city which it considers a necessary operational loss.
