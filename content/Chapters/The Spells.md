## Overview

### Aïnhaz Spells
To cast aïnhaz spells, one must have an intuitive connection to the flows of the world around them.  The haid and agïs of a place are difficult to shape into forms which are not like that which originated them.  As such, aïnhaz spells are typically not named after particular spellcasters, or even given names.  Instead, they are categorized by the biotope they are cast from, and the skill of the caster (and their own agïs) determines how much haid may be siphoned into the spell.

Mechanically, aïnhaz spellcasting is a build-your-spell experience wherein available effects are determined by where you are physically in the world and damage and effect scales with spell level.  The focus of the grand majority of aïnhaz spells is to *have effects* rather than *deal damage* but there are a handful which channel enormous energies predominately for damage.  These are typically no more powerful than an equivalent arcane spell, but the flexibility of aïnhaz casting can allow a skilled or creative caster to deal more damage by tailoring which saves are required or where attacks come from.

With few exceptions, aïnhaz spells can only be cast if the requisite physical conditions are met.  Storm spells require stormy weather, cave spells require caves, etc.  Edge cases (e.g. there's no thunder but I wish to cast storm spells) are typically determined by roleplay.
