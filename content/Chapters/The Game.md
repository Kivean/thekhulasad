## Overview
Here begins the more player focused sections of the Khulasad.  The trappings of a Tarjali academic field reference will largely be discarded, and the effort from hereon out will be providing an effective quick reference for purposes of actually playing a tabletop.  

The game will be running in Pathfinder, specifically whatever the Pathfinder rules included in the d20PFSRD are.  The d20PFSRD is accessible at https://www.d20pfsrd.com/.  Wherever the rules I homebrew disagree with the d20PFSRD, I will probably default to either my rules or whatever is cooler.  The only areas where I will include totally novel content are in *The Classes*, *The Spells*, *The Feats*, and *The Monsters*.  For all other mechanical matters, the d20PFSRD will be the reference.  

d20PFSRD is notoriously verbose.  Its easy to get lost in the complexity, and they don't make it easier by having the words "core", "base", and "standard" used with abandon to refer to different things.  Start with the **Core and Base Classes** and the **Core and Featured Races** and then work from there.  I am not adding any additional races unless requested.  *I am also not married to any of the racial lore in Pathfinder.  Find a cool race and see a place it could fit into the story?  Let me know and we can easily retcon the Pathfinder lore and make this world more interesting.*

Many of the Hybrid and Occult classes are awesome too, but again, its easy to get lost.  Please don't worry too much about combat balance, I will adjust encounters to make the game fun and to challenge the party.  3rd-party rules are on a case-by-case basis because some of them are broken as fuck.  Ask me if you have questions!  

The *goal* of this setting is to provide a totally interoperable pathfinder setting *mechanically*, but not in terms of *lore*.  This means that despite my best efforts to create a setting with interesting deities, notable historical figures, new balanced classes, and dastardly villains, there will inevitably be hundreds of things from the established Pathfinder rules which are not-at-all lore compliant in this setting.  The spell, *Janni's Jaunt*, for example, will be named oddly without a lore compliant legendary mage named Janni.  Well, I simply don't have the time to rewrite an *entire* setting, so we'll all have to suspend our disbelief when this comes up.  By all means, feel free to bring it up and test my improvisational skills as I try to cram something into the lore.

Finally, my general house rules apply:
- **Rule of Cool**: I'll always try to let you do something if its sick as hell.  I will go out of my way to reward creativity unless it is explicitly out of the scope of some ability, or would be game breaking (i.e. it'll leave me with fuckall to do in the session).
- **Anyone Can Be Anything**: DND-based tabletop settings are notorious for having some, uh, dated ideas about who and what can do things.  You want to be a lawful-good gnoll? Cool with me.  I'll do my best to make sure there's little friction based on race, gender, etc.  If you want to play a race or creature that, say, doesn't have eyes or is 30 feet tall, that might cause some problems, but you're welcome to do it.  This is partly why I am not adding additional races.
- **Combat Counts**: I try to make combats *mean* something, which, in turn, means everything else has to mean more, since most of the rules in Pathfinder are about combat.  Thaja is by no means a peaceful world, most of its citizens are equipped for violence at any given time, but this doesn't mean violence between people is common.  There will always be consequences for violence.  
- **Roleplay Above Rolls**: Roleplay is awesome, and super rewarding!  I try to let us *tell* the story, and let the dice *spin* that story.  If you're trying to lie to someone, lie first, *then we roll*.  If you tell a great lie at the table, the DC for that roll will be adjusted lower.  I find this helps to tell the best story, while still allowing for the dice to fuck stuff up.  Its funny when you have a great lie and roll a 1, its not funny when you tell a great lie at the table and then I penalize you for not rolling above a 25.  

## Campaign Primer
It was an unremarkable day.  In the lives of some, unremarkable days see violence and trial.  Others have nothing to speak of save the pleasant mundanity long cultivated by a life of leisure.  The Tarjali farmers call spans of these days *mustaq tines,* "straight weeks" where one knows their trajectory from the start.  

To be found by a stranger is unsettling in proportion to one's isolation and usually inversely to one's renown.  The stranger that found you that day was also unremarkable, though their arrival may have been a surprise.  The letter in their hands, *printed* on real paper in several languages, was what turned that unremarkable day into the start of something else entirely.  

Aside from the effort put into its construction (could it really have been typeset by an *ad'fahum?*), the content of the letter was, at first, unremarkable too.  Greetings, we heard your name from so-and-so (some *wazzil* who knows someone you know) who told us about a recent deed of yours (in some detail, it seemed).  We think you would be a good fit for our work.  Payment of a nominal sum for travel to Topaz and presentation for further evaluation. 

Many adventurers of any renown have received such a letter.  A few, even, might receive one printed so handsomely.  You rolled your eyes.  Kept reading.  Raised your eyebrows on the next page.  Opened your eyes wider near the end, then fought the impulse to burn the thing.

How did they hear about *that?*  Certainly their so-and-so couldn't have heard about that business, or any related business besides.  And the nerve to mention it, even vaguely alluded to, in a written letter!  The private courier now made sense.  There was no mention of a consequence or punishment for failing to comply, but certainly there was no need.  If that business got out, well, let the old Tarjali parable speak true. *Let that which grows in shade, stay in shade. Sun scorches both growth and grower.*

So you returned to reading, made arrangements, and packed your necessities for an undefined stay in Topaz.  Lodgings would be provided, apparently, at the 38th *Nikab* of Topaz.  *Which Nikab?* you thought, *I know the 37th publishes the Edelasad...*  The rest of the letter was vague on what was actually going on, but it seemed you were being asked to test some sort of scientific theory.

The journey was about as miserable as could be expected.  *Qatila* were delayed, weather was bad, and your arrival in Topaz was a relief.  Ah the Jewel of the Tavilla, ever a stunning place to arrive. Stone grown into fantastic monolithic constructions and enormous, crystal clear panes of glass.  Colorful flows of Tarjali bustling around quieter parks where roving philosophers are ignored or debated by passersby in equal measure. Books and book shops everywhere, fed by the low, armored caravans of paper from forests abroad.  

Asking for the 38th *Nikab* got you directed to a complex of tightly packed academic-looking structures near *Suja.*  Confused, you shouldered your way past a crowd of Jaks practicing odd jerky steps (apparently the latest development in *wathuud*) to find a set of buildings labeled the 39th *Nikab.*  Going the other way, you found the decorated door of the 37th *Nikab* where you were nearly run over by a sharp squad of *Nikab* students carrying an enormous, shuddering box.  After some minutes of searching what seemed like back alleys, you finally came across a shaded courtyard accessed through a narrow, curiously circular gated door. Within, a curiously armored door and a proud stone sign: the 38th *Nikab.*

Your ring was answered promptly, and a succession of friendly, *Nikab* staff took you inside, subjected you to several magical assessments, deprived you of weapons and belongings (to be found in your room, you were assured) confirmed your identity, signed forms in triplicate, introduced you to research staff, took your measurements, and finally paid you and put you, thoroughly spun about, to bed.  

Despite the days oddities, its rigors made sleep come easy.  In the morning you were met at your door by two people you hadn't seen the day prior.  The first was a slight Tarjali scholar in oddly Uqasaq-looking dress named Proctor Shalludh, the lead researcher for "the project" you'd heard so much about.  The second was a tall, stoutly built Sahida whose *saj,* noticeably colored with *tlel,* hung collapsed at her hip.  She was introduced as Ilya Prem, and she said nothing as she surveyed you by eye.

The two conveyed you to a light breakfast, where Proctor Shalludh adroitly avoided the usual Tarjali pleasantries by saying, simply, "You will have questions, I'm sure."

Shalludh explained, finally, and in shockingly straightforward terms, why you had been brought here and poked and prodded.

"We here at the 38th are specialists in the growth and maintenance of flesh.  While the 37th creates *edalires*, and the 39th creates *fahum,* we have a more refined goal in mind.  We are on their campus for the use of some of their expertise and facilities, but as you can see from our surrounds, our work is more dangerous."

You hadn't thought it prudent to remark on the heavily fortified doors and walls, nor the occasional tang of powerful wards which would be apparent to even the most haid-blind.  Shalludh waved away any comment you may have been preparing.

"Ordinarily we accept few visitors, but your situation is special.  You have proven yourself both proficient and profane, and therefore malleable."

"What the Proctor is saying," remarked the Sahida in a deep, dry tone, "is that their shameless blackmail is not an insult, but an opportunity for reward."

Proctor Shalludh nodded without the slightest hint of embarrassment. "Indeed.  We have a technology which requires testing, and you have the skills to test it.  We have the leverage to prevent you from disclosing it.  The Sahida here," Prem sighed prematurely, "are pleased with our early results, even if they don't approve of all of our methods."

"We have, put plainly, developed the ability to produce a copy of your body.  This copy, we call them *nukhat jin jisn,* or *ideal ve-*"

"A perfect copy." Cut in the Sahida.

"Yes," Salludh was only mildly flustered, "our technology is very good.  We produce a perfect copy of your body.  It is so perfect, in fact, that you should experience no discomfort when you inhabit it."

Before you could register the impact of this statement, the Proctor continued.

"Of course, you have only one soul.  While you do not inhabit this body, it is capable only of the most rudimentary of functions: breathing, circulation, digestion, urinary function, bowel fu-" The Proctor paused, catching a look from the Sahida, and blinked twice before continuing.  "That is to say it is not really *you* unless *your soul* is inside of it.  We have also mastered a horizontal transfer of the soul from body to body, but that isn't germane to this discussion.  The point is, we have provided a method by which anyone has the ability to proceed safely into dangerous situations.  How much do you know about the soul?"

You opened your mouth but the Proctor continued without waiting.  "In a resurrection, much of the trouble is first reconstructing the body so that the soul has somewhere to go, but the larger issue is, of course, pulling the soul, which now believes its body destroyed, back to the body.  Certain techniques and strikes of monks can temporarily eject the soul from the body, but it always returns.  Why?  Because the soul is attracted to an in-tact body through a force akin to magnetism.  Our horizontal soul transfer leaves your original body alive and in the same stasis we keep our *nukhat jin jisn,* so if you were to be killed, your soul ought to snap back to your original body without a cleric's assistance."

"If 'ought' isn't enough confidence for you, please know we are very confident that this will work.  In fact, we are staking the reputation of our entire *Nikab* on it.  You can imagine why this would be a powerful technology, especially for those with dangerous lives."

With the technical explanation exhausted, Shalludh's commentary awkwardly halted.  You had the decency to inquire the usual questions.  Why me again? What do you actually need me to do? What is the payment and what is the risk? 

This time, the Sahida spoke. "We will need you to remain here for a few weeks while your copy is grown.  During this time you will meet the others we have contacted for the same purpose.  Then your soul will be transferred into the copy and you will all be sent on a few errands with only a nominal danger.  You will return and report on the functions of the copies and your experiences.  Then you'll be paid handsomely and sent on you way."

There was a pause where Proctor Shalludh clearly wanted to say something, and Ilya Prem spoke again, somewhat begrudgingly.  

"Should you attempt to abscond with your body, or tell anyone else about it, or otherwise jeopardize the operation, Shalludh here will provide the details on your prior transgressions to myself and several of my Wren comrades, who will then be bound by Sahida code to render justice.  Whatever that may look like.  Satisfied?"

The last question was directed at Proctor Shalludh, who nodded cheerfully.  "I highly doubt that will be a problem though.  We had this cleared by the Ethicists of the 15th *Nikab,* and they all gave excellent prognostics about your likelihood to comply."

Shalludh had a sip of tea, and then interrupted it to add, "Oh and now that you know all this, i'm afraid you're not allowed to go anywhere until we make your *nukhat jin jisn* and clear you for your first outing."

"I think that was made clear..." Prem said, wryly.  
###### For Players

**WELCOME, players,** to the grounds of the 38th *Nikab* and their latest experiment.  As you can see, you are not playing a freshly-born, bushy-tailed (are tails usually bushy when things are born?) adventurer, but someone of high enough renown to have done *something* but of low enough importance to have *gotten away* with something else.  

We will begin at **level 3.**  For arms and armor to start with, I will refer you to the D20PFSRD item list.  You can roll *starting wealth* according to your class page and multiply by four.  You can also take (for free) the cheapest armor at any given armor class (e.g. splint mail in the +7 bouns range) and any un-enchanted weapon you'd like.  Because you also have some deeds behind you, you also get one *magic item.* You may take any **lesser minor** item worth 3000 gp or less (this includes simple +1 weapons or armor.)  To find general information on equipment go here: https://www.d20pfsrd.com/equipment/  To find magical items you may refer here: https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic-items/  

I have hopefully allowed a nearly infinite set of backgrounds and experiences, but I will require the following things from you to flesh out your character and provide grounds for early roleplay!  There are two categories of thing: things everyone will know, and things you can choose to tell other players.  
###### Things you can *choose* to tell other players
- **One or more heroic deeds,** something consummate with level 3, and preferably related loosely to adventuring.  
- **How your got your magic item,** this can be related to your heroic deed, but preferably something more interesting than "I bought it cause I had money from killing some goblins."
- **One or more dastardly deeds,** something serious enough that you wouldn't want it getting out that you were involved.  You can choose whether or not you're ashamed of this, falsely-but-credibly accused, or whatnot.  The only thing *required* is that its enough to blackmail you.
- **What were you doing when the letter came?** What it says on the tin, related to next bullet.
- **A brief backstory,** amnesiacs allowed if desired.  What was your life like?
- **Your deity, deities, or spiritual tradition.**  If this is important to your character, put more sauce on it.
- **A skill or skills you're good at.** Not the on-a-character-sheet skills, but things you do or did in your life.  Maybe you were a carpenter *before* becoming the son of god, or whatnot.  Or perhaps you still practice carpentry during your son of god days.  Either way, carpentry is a skill.
###### Things other players *will know*
- **Three rumors about you,** two of these should be true, one should be false.  You'll tell *only me* which ones are true, but everyone will hear the three rumors.
- **Your appearance,** do art or write a description or do a fun photoshoot, whatever you want.  Ideally this will include what you typically wear, what languages you speak and with what accent, colors and styles you might be seen in, all the things people might notice in the first 15 seconds.
- **Your demeanor,** are you friendly? Loud? Reserved? Flirty?  This is the way other people will perceive you when acting normally.  If you act differently alone or around friends, that's excellent, but this is your "public facing" demeanor.  

These bits of information flesh out your character in addition to your character sheet.  You can write me *as much of this as you like* and I will read it all because I am a nerd.  At minimum, two sentences for each of these.  Three for the backstory.  Sorry to assign homework.
