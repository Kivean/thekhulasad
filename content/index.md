## Introduction
Welcome, players, to the world of [[The World#Thaja|Thaja]] and her many colorful denizens and cultures. Across her large, sparsely populated surface are, I hope, endless avenues for adventure, conflict, companionship, and humor.  I have done my best to compile all of the game-critical lore in as readable a form as possible in this, English, referred to throughout the text as "Low Common".  Wherever possible, I have tried to leave ample room for varied interpretations and diversity in culture and custom.  Within and in addition to the core lore, there are words in five languages and a depth of (eventually) plot relevant lore, mostly presented through the lens of the [[The People#The Tarjal|Tarjali]] academia.  Their own idiosyncrasies permeate the text, which is fitting considering you will spend a good portion of the campaign (at least for a while) among them.  There are already puzzles (small and large) to be solved or have their solutions found within this text, and I intend to include more.  The hope is that this document will live and grow with us and the game.  

 The first puzzle, I think, will be getting used to how information is distributed.  Different facts and anecdotes about topics are spread about the document, in part to encourage cross-referencing and clue hunting, and in part because reproducing each fact in every section which might mention that topic would make this 300 times longer.  For example, the [[The People#The Sahida|Sahida]] wear the *mahatalli*, which is described mechanically in [[The Classes#Sahida|The Classes]], defined in the [[index#Glossary|Glossary]], and discussed in [[The People#The Sahida|The People]].  I encourage digging into The Khulasad to explore.

With that in mind, what is The Khulasad?  The Khulasad, compiled continuously by the *Jaks* and *Katibeh* of the 1st *Nikab* of [[The Land#Topaz|Topaz]], strives to be a dictionary, wayfarers guide, encyclopedia, and general-education textbook all in one.  The Tarjali are quite didactic, and unlike their neighbors, prefer to use Tarjali as their language of science, instead of using established terms in High Common.  As a result of the need to be uniquely comprehensive, The Khulasad (commonly distributed in Low Common), remains one of the best references of its kind.

As you can see, there are *italicized words* which are in other languages (Tarjali, in this case) which are glossed in the [[index#Glossary|Glossary]]  Links, such as the one to the Glossary, work as they do in wikipedia, but you cannot click an italicized word to get to its glossary entry, and for this I am sorry.  I *will* forget to italicize some words, please forgive me in advance.

In areas it is useful for me to convey information which the Tarjali would not, for a variety of reasons, believe or share.  Where this happens, [[The People#Fad e Wurds|Wurder]] Shab, who owned this copy of The Khulasad before us, will include his handwritten addenda, in the form shown below:

> This copy of the 56th revision of The Khulasad is the SOLE PROPERTY of Hakem Jekivny Shab, war-scholar of the Highest Convent of Bangjaluk, soon-to-be Prelate, and first among fellows!  Included are my tabulated notes, IN THIS FORMAT, from my copies of the 50th, 47th, and 40th revisions of The Khulasad.  If you are reading this, you are a thief and a bastard, undoubtedly of weak liver and small mind.  Return this book AT ONCE or SUFFER THE CONSEQUENCES.
> 
> ADDENDUM: Think me not a Tarjali lest my shame bury us both!

Shab has his own views and biases, and for flavor (and fun) not all of his commentary will be of use, I'm sure you'll get to learn what kind of person he is.  

I'd like to make a few crucial notes with regards to this document and hopefully encourage some collaborativity in the worldbuilding.
1. First and foremost, this document *will* grow as we play.  I will make every attempt to contain new lore we create, include new tidbits, or point you all in the correct direction on puzzles or lore challenges.  BUT: until the game officially begins, *anything may change*.
2. The good: anything may change!  Don't like something?  Want to see something added?  Caught a mistake?  Let's chat!  I am always in need of inspiration, so if you throw me an idea, whether nebulous or specific, it'll be a joy for me to fit it into the world here.  It is a pocket goal of mine that each of you will end up, in some respect, an expert in some part of the lore, such that sessions can be even more interactive.
3. The bad: this all has to end somewhere.  Once we begin the game, for playability, I will strive to avoid retconning lore or plot as much as possible.  I will always be excited to add new dimensions, and I don't mind modifying lore a bit to squeeze more interesting ideas in.  I *especially* don't mind changing class, feat, and spell balancing to create enjoyable gameplay.  At some level, however, there's some core worldbuilding that can only change if we have an in-world catastrophe or paradigm shift which truly changes everything.  

Finally, I am instituting a rule I'm calling the **No Bullshit Rule**. I know that I'm an insufferably wordy writer, and that the limitations of an "in-world" lore reference may generate some frustration, especially where the available narrators contradict each other or appear to contradict in-game phenomena as we play.  **If this happens and you're getting frustrated, you may always ask for a No Bullshit explanation of whatever is going on**.  If you do this, I will give you the clearest, omniscient explanation I can give without spoiling plot.  If, for example, you're trying to workshop the backstory for a character from a Jazak speaking tribe, it might be annoying as hell to have only the Tarjali perspective on their life and culture.  Ask for a no bullshit explanation and I'll tell you what the Jazak are actually like.  More likely, I'll tell you a rough idea of what the Jazak *could* look like, and let you build lore that helps us both tell the kind of story you want to play.

The chapters from The World to The People are all presented in the form of the Khulasad itself.  Beyond that, the chapters from The Game to The Monsters will be presented as more of a rulebook.  The order you chose to read things is up to you, but I'd recommend reading the [[The Game#Overview|Overview]] section of the chapter The Game first, before digging into the lore.  It's not long, and will give you a general idea of my approach to the core pathfinder lore and ruleset.   

Let's have some fun and dig into a world a little more colorful than our own.  Along the way I'm sure we'll find a way to bring those colors out into real life.

As the Tarjali would say: *One may smell every sea, may ford every river, but one can best see the whole of Thaja in a single thirsty drop*.

## Glossary
Most words glossed are [[The People#The Tarjal|Tarjali]], with equivalent terms in [[The People#The Coastal Empires|Uqasaq]] or occasionally [[The People#Steppe Peoples|Jazak]] provided inline for quick reference.  Words in [[The People#The Halliger Remnants|High Common]] are provided only where they continue to be relevant.  If plurals have not adopted the Low Common conventions (an s after the word indicates a plural, e.g. boarj(s)), plurals are given after a word in parentheses e.g. katib(eh).  For a complete dictionary of Low Common words and phrases, the authors recommend the Muejasad compiled by the 6th Nikab of Topaz.

Links go to other pages, not within the glossary, but all words should be defined.  For example the link [[The People#Fad e Wurds|wurder]] will bring you to the page detailing the Fad e Wurds but there is also a glossary entry for wurder.  As the glossary is intended for quick reference, it typically includes "field-relevant" descriptions or information, while other pages go deeper into the lore.
